# The Register of Durable Things

Research note, August 2026. Human activities continuous across 1026 → 2026, and
likely to reach 3026.

Published as an artifact: https://claude.ai/code/artifact/249e483c-da41-46a0-9547-41aba59ae58c

## The screening test

Old is not the same as durable. Plenty of things were already a thousand years old
in 1026 and are gone now. To predict forward rather than just inventory backward, an
activity has to pass four filters.

1. **Anchored in a body that hasn't changed.** ~2,000 kcal/day, a third of life
   asleep, nine months' gestation, twenty years to a finished brain. Anthropology's
   catalogue of ~400 human universals (Brown, 1991) is mostly downstream of that
   hardware.
2. **Defined by its purpose, not its equipment.** A durable activity re-implements
   itself in whatever technology is available. If you can only describe it by naming
   its tool, it is a method — and methods die.
3. **Nobody has to organise it.** It regrows after suppression and happens without
   institutions.
4. **It already has the track record.** The Lindy prior: for things with no natural
   lifespan, expected remaining life runs roughly proportional to current age.

## The register — eighteen things that held

| Activity | 1026 | 2026 | 3026 projection | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Eating together | One pot, shared trencher, bread and ale; feast days structure the calendar | Kitchen table, restaurant, potluck | Synthesis changes the menu, not the meal; commensality predates the species | Near-certain |
| Telling stories | Skald in the hall, saints' lives, tales at the fire — almost all oral | Novels, film, serials, podcasts, group chat | Medium unguessable, appetite fixed; individual plots already 6,000 years old | Near-certain |
| Music and moving to it | Work songs, plainchant, pipe and drum at harvest | All recorded music — and people still gather to hear it live | Beat entrainment is neurological; infants have it pre-language | Near-certain |
| Playing games | Dice, tables (backgammon's ancestor), chess newly arrived, wrestling | Backgammon, chess, go, mancala — same rules — plus screens | Strongest empirical case: these exact games already crossed a millennium | Near-certain |
| Gossiping | The well, the mill, the church porch | Group chat, feed, smoking area | Reputation-tracking is what a social species does; channel is incidental | Near-certain |
| Raising children | Mother's work, then field or apprenticeship | Two decades of formalised dependency | A brain that takes 20 years to finish sets the term | Near-certain |
| Courting | Negotiated match, dowry, the occasional love affair against it | Apps, algorithms, marriage as a repeated choice | Forms churn every century; the activity underneath never has | Near-certain |
| Mourning the dead | Wake, grave goods, anniversary mass paid years ahead | Funeral, obituary, the memorial page nobody deletes | No known society without death rites; unseated only by unseating death | High, conditional |

## The hard anchors

- **12,000 years / ~500 generations** — Cloggs Cave, Victoria: two miniature fireplaces,
  each holding a fat-smeared, tip-burned Casuarina stick, matching a ritual GunaiKurnai
  elders still describe and 19th-c ethnographers recorded. Longest continuously
  transmitted cultural practice yet documented. *(Nature Human Behaviour, 2024)*
- **6,000 years** — "The Smith and the Devil" traced to the Bronze Age by phylogenetic
  analysis across Indo-European languages; Jack-and-the-Beanstalk type ~5,000. Not
  storytelling in general — these particular plots. *(da Silva & Tehrani, R. Soc. Open Sci., 2016)*
- **5,000 years** — Backgammon: playable by a Mesopotamian scribe, a Roman emperor, a
  crusader, Pepys in 1663, and you tonight, with no rules negotiation.
- **3,800 years** — The oldest surviving written recipe is the Hymn to Ninkasi, and it
  is for beer. Chemical evidence for brewing runs to ~13,000 years, possibly predating farming.
- **1,900 years** — Vindolanda: a woman writes to her "dearest sister" inviting her to a
  birthday party. Among the earliest known Latin handwriting by a woman.
- **11,000 inscriptions** — Pompeii: love, election slogans, boasts, prices, bad jokes,
  "Secundinus, the shitter." Every category of internet writing, fully formed, sealed in 79 AD.

## The counter-record — things that looked just as permanent and broke

| Practice | 1026 | 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Memorising and performing epic | The principal delivery system for literature | Specialist survival; mass literacy removed the job |
| Subsistence farming | What the overwhelming majority of everyone who ever lived did all day | Low single-digit % of industrialised workforces |
| Hand-spinning and hand-laundering | Among the largest consumers of human labour-hours in history, borne by women | Machines, universally |
| Copying books by hand | A thousand-year-old institution at its peak | Gone within a century of movable type |
| Execution as public entertainment | Well-attended, festival atmosphere, food sellers | Abolished or hidden nearly everywhere |

**The rule that falls out: the durable unit is the verb, not the tool.** Every register
entry is a want. Every counter-record entry is a method. Nothing on the counter-record
died because people stopped needing what it delivered — with the single instructive
exception of the scaffold, where the appetite itself was extinguished.

So the failure mode in projecting a millennium is *describing an activity at the altitude
of its equipment*. "Reading a printed novel" is a method with maybe a few centuries of
form left. "Following a story someone made up" is a want with a hundred thousand years
behind it.

## Risk register — what could break the durable list

- **Death decoupled from biology** — unseats mourning, inheritance, generational succession.
- **Reproduction decoupled from bodies** — changes childbirth; child-*rearing* is safer,
  since the 20-year window is set by brain development, not the womb.
- **Synthetic companionship** — *the only risk already underway in 2026.* If artificial
  company substitutes for the social functions of gossip, courtship and storytelling,
  three of the strongest entries weaken at once.
- **Convincing virtual presence** — pilgrimage and travel rest on physically being
  somewhere mattering.
- **Post-scarcity** — craft and trade lose their necessity anchor, though both already
  survived losing it once.
- **Divergence of the species** — if "human" stops naming one population, "human universal"
  stops being a coherent category.

Note what these have in common: almost none are cultural. **This is not really a prediction
about culture — it is a prediction about the body.** If a person in 3026 has roughly the
metabolism, lifespan, developmental timetable and social cognition of a person in 1026,
essentially the whole register holds. Every genuine uncertainty lives in that conditional.

## The bet, ranked

1. Eating together — predates the species
2. Telling and following stories — individual plots already 6,000 years old
3. Gossiping — needs no institution to survive
4. Music and moving to a beat — neurological, pre-linguistic
5. Playing games — the same games already crossed 1,000 years
6. Raising children — a twenty-year brain sets the term
7. Courting and pair-bonding — forms churn, activity doesn't
8. Decorating the body — everyone is issued the surface
9. Marking the year's turn — a cycle always gets a mark
10. Trading and haggling — on every universals list ever compiled
11. Complaining about the young — 4,000 years of unbroken attestation
12. Mourning the dead — safe unless death is

## Coda: reading did not make the list

Writing is ~5,000 years old. Mass literacy ~200. The printed codex ~570. The novel is
younger; the paperback is younger than television. By the four filters, reading is a
**method** — a superb one, but equipment.

What it is a method *for* sits at number two on the bet list with a hundred millennia
behind it. And the thing people do right after finishing a story they loved — find
someone and tell them to read it — fuses the second and third register entries.
Recommending a story to another person is older than the book, older than writing, and
almost certainly older than any technology that will ever carry it.

The durable asset was never the format. It was the appetite, and the talking about it
afterwards.
